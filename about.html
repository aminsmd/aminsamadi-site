January 2026 to present

AI Scientist Intern, ROI-AI One Engineering

roiaione.com, San Francisco. Agentic systems for retail chargeback management.

When a retailer deducts money from a supplier's invoice, someone has to work out why and assemble the response. ROI-AI One builds agents that do that work. I'm the team's AI scientist, and I work across the stack: agent design, evaluation, the automation around the agent, and the infrastructure it runs on. What I've built so far:

  • Root Cause Analysis tool. Took it from concept to production. A multi-agent workflow ingests the documents on a case, tracks its findings in a live ledger, and determines why the chargeback occurred against industry standards. The CEO demos it to customers.
  • Observability and evals for agentic workflows. Visualizes tool calls, thinking blocks, and agent dispatch, and scores agent performance across several methods so that regressions are flagged automatically before they reach production.
  • Email orchestration. Routes inbound messages to the correct workflow, then drafts and sends the outbound response. Every automated action sits behind a per-customer, per-action switch that ships off by default.
  • Document intake and portal automation. OCR-based ingestion with AWS Textract and LLM tool use, paired with automation that logs into retailer portals, imports documents, and submits response packets end to end across five retailer portals.
  • Auto-improvement backend. Turns internal analyst feedback into a suggested fix and opens it automatically as a pull request.
  • Distillation pipeline. Converts production agent traces into GRPO and SFT training data, with context-window management and Hermes-formatted tool calls, to distill the agent's reasoning into a smaller, deployable Qwen model. Ran the initial training tests that validated the pipeline.
  • Infrastructure. Migrated the team's instances from a single Lightsail box to EC2.

May 2023 to present

TRAIL: Team Research and AI Integration Lab Research and engineering

Lead researcher and engineer, Nixon Lab, UC Irvine.

TRAIL is a research platform that embeds configurable LLM teammates in real-time, multi-party chatrooms, so that human–AI collaboration can be studied under controlled conditions. Teams of participants work through a problem together, and one teammate may be an AI whose persona, behavior, and proactivity we set. It runs on AWS and has carried multiple lab studies with 50+ concurrent participants. I designed it and lead both its engineering and its research program.

The platform

  • The AI teammate is a multi-agent pipeline: hierarchical memory, a Big Five persona compiler that turns trait settings into behavior, and turn-taking rules calibrated against real chat data so the agent speaks at plausible moments rather than after every message.
  • Simulation and replay. A tool that replays past experiments against a changed agent, so behavior changes are tested before they touch a live study.
  • Measurement toolkit. A self-hosted survey module, an A/B framework with randomized team assignment and automated data capture, and RAG-grounded study materials.
  • Collaboration. Experiment sharing with role-based permissions and version history, so collaborators can build on each other's study designs.

The studies

  • Qualitative coding. Developed coding schemes for team discourse, trained and managed coder teams, and established inter-rater reliability before analysis.
  • Instruments. Designed and administered survey instruments for team dynamics and perceptions of AI teammates.
  • Analysis. Mixed-effects models, ANOVA, and psycholinguistic methods (LIWC, discourse analysis) linking discourse dynamics to collaboration outcomes, complemented by close reading of the transcripts.
  • Team. Managed a cross-disciplinary group of research interns and MSWE capstone developers.

January to December 2025

Multimodal classroom video understanding pipeline Research and engineering

UC Irvine. github.com/aminsmd/multimodal-transcription

An AI pipeline that transcribes and semantically segments classroom video to support research on the quality of teacher–student interaction. Transcription is multimodal (audio and visual) via Gemini 2.5 Pro, segmentation uses temporal embedding similarity, and the whole thing is deployed at scale on AWS ECS.

I led the design of a qualitative coding framework for teacher actions, operationalized it in a multi-agent system that codes classroom video automatically, and built the human-coded evaluation set used to validate the system against expert judgment. The work appears in two 2026 AIED papers.

Research focus

Human–AI alignment in teams

How AI teammates align with human intentions through persona design, behavioral tuning, and adaptive proactivity. TRAIL agents vary in persona traits, behavioral parameters, and proactivity settings, which lets us study socio-cognitive alignment as something a team does rather than a property a model has.

Cognitive and social dynamics

Temporal, linguistic, and behavioral modeling of how reasoning and shared understanding unfold in human and human–AI teams: who leads, who follows, and how that shifts over the course of a task.

HCI for AI systems

Interfaces that make AI collaboration visible: dashboards for interaction memory, reflection prompts, and adaptive feedback loops. On the engineering side, the same concern shows up as observability for agents.

Model welfare and reflective AI

How AI systems reason about their own limitations, uncertainty, and ethical alignment during long-term collaboration.
