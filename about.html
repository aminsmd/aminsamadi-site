January to September 2026

AI Scientist Intern, ROIAI One Engineering

roiaione.com, San Francisco. Agentic AI for B2B financial claims.

When a retailer deducts money from a supplier's invoice, someone has to work out whether the deduction is valid, dispute it if not, and stop it from happening again. ROIAI One builds agents that do that work. I was the team's AI scientist and worked across the stack, from agent design to the infrastructure it runs on. What I built:

  • Root cause analysis workflow. A multi-agent system using GraphRAG and long-term agent memory (key-value state plus free-form notes) that determines who is at fault for a claim and how to prevent it, grounded in policy documents and domain-expert knowledge. The founder demoed it to customers, and it led to a paid pilot.
  • Core dispute-automation agent. The original agent that processes a chargeback end to end: claim extraction, evidence gathering from ERP systems and email, verification against shipment records, and dispute-email drafting. It replaced a manual analyst workflow. I maintained its skills and agent configuration after the move to a Claude Agent SDK flow.
  • Agent observability and evals. A tracing system that visualizes tool calls, thinking blocks, and agent dispatch across multi-agent workflows, and an eval dashboard that scores agent performance across multiple methods.
  • Portal automation. Logs into retailer portals, imports documents, and submits response packets, across five retailer portals.
  • Self-improvement loop (prototype). Turns internal analyst feedback into a suggested fix, opened automatically as a pull request.
  • Agentic post-training pipeline (prototype). Converts agent traces into GRPO and SFT data, with context-window management and Hermes-formatted tool-call trajectories, to distill the agent's reasoning into a small Qwen model. Ran the initial training experiments.
  • Infrastructure. Prompt caching, LLM cost tracking, queue-based workers, and the migration from a single Lightsail instance to EC2.
  • Customers. Onboarded four customer accounts and ran their weekly meetings: status updates, action items, and issue follow-up.

May 2023 to present

TRAIL: Team Research and AI Integration Lab Research and engineering

Lead engineer and researcher, UC Irvine. trail.aminsamadi.org

TRAIL is a real-time, multi-party chat platform that embeds configurable LLM teammates, built so that human–AI collaboration can be studied under controlled conditions. Teams of participants work through a problem together, and one teammate may be an AI whose persona and behavior we set. I designed it, lead its engineering and its studies, and manage a cross-disciplinary team of research interns and MSWE capstone developers.

The platform

  • Stack. Django, Flask-SocketIO, Next.js and React, PostgreSQL, Redis, and Docker with AWS CI/CD, load-tested for 50+ concurrent participants.
  • The AI teammate is a four-stage agent pipeline: message batching, memory integration, a speak-or-stay-silent decision, and persona-aligned generation with graded Big Five personas, using multi-provider models through the OpenAI and Gemini APIs.
  • Memory and retrieval. Two-tier agent memory, short-term context plus long-term reflective summaries that re-anchor generation to counter persona drift, and RAG over attached documents with logged retrieval.
  • Simulation and replay. A tool that tests prompt and behavior changes against past experiments before deployment.
  • Reliability. An explicit lifecycle state machine with restart recovery, role-based access, and consent tracking.
  • Experiment infrastructure. Seeded randomized assignment, chained multi-session studies, per-message decision-trace export, and a separate analytics microservice that measures mimicry, turn-taking balance, and participation inequality.

The studies

  • Classroom deployment. A six-session classroom study with about 51 students and 4,495 messages, in which the agent contributed roughly 30% of messages without dominating the conversation.
  • Pilot RCT. Human-only versus human–AI teams, which surfaced differences in inclusivity, peer learning, and trust.
  • Persona fidelity. Evaluated GPT-4o, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, Gemini 2.5 Pro, and Grok-3 with a standard personality inventory over 32 trait configurations, plus linguistic analysis of dialogue and memory. Agent memory amplified trait signals (preprint, 2026).

Multimodal video understanding pipeline Research and engineering

UC Irvine. github.com/aminsmd/multimodal-transcription

A Gemini 2.5 Pro multimodal transcription pipeline for classroom video, so that education researchers can analyze classroom interaction at scale: video chunking, structured prompts for verbatim speech, speaker roles, and visual events, transcript merging, and automated validation. Deployed to AWS ECS Fargate with GitHub Actions CI/CD, Terraform-managed stage and prod environments, and S3 outputs.

  • Benchmarks. Compared against Whisper, GPT-4o-Transcribe, and an audio-only Gemini ablation on 17 middle-school math videos (about 8 hours) and 30 public ClassBank videos (about 6 hours). On the math classrooms it had the lowest word error rate (0.14 vs. 0.29, 0.30, and 0.16) and under 2.5% speaker, content, and boundary errors, where GPT-4o misattributed 11.9% and omitted 22% of utterances. Whisper remained best on the ClassBank set (WER 0.26 vs. 0.39).
  • Evaluation. Designed and led the human-rated benchmark process (evaluation plan, video and segment sampling strategy); the qualitative coding itself was done by trained education researchers.
  • Multi-agent coder. Turned a teaching-practice observation rubric into a multi-agent coder and scorer (event extraction, coding, scoring), evaluated on 82 human-coded segments: visual context raised precision from 0.79 to 0.85 and cut false positives from 17.3% to 12.9%. Co-first author on one AIED 2026 paper and a contributor to a second.

Research focus

Human–AI alignment in teams

How AI teammates align with human intentions through persona design, behavioral tuning, and adaptive proactivity. TRAIL agents vary in persona traits, behavioral parameters, and proactivity settings, which lets us study socio-cognitive alignment as something a team does rather than a property a model has.

Cognitive and social dynamics

Temporal, linguistic, and behavioral modeling of how reasoning and shared understanding unfold in human and human–AI teams: who leads, who follows, and how that shifts over the course of a task.

HCI for AI systems

Interfaces that make AI collaboration visible: dashboards for interaction memory, reflection prompts, and adaptive feedback loops. On the engineering side, the same concern shows up as observability for agents.

Model welfare and reflective AI

How AI systems reason about their own limitations, uncertainty, and ethical alignment during long-term collaboration.
